\begin{abstract}
An autonomous AI-agent research program had certified a proportion bound
$N_0(T)/N(T) \ge 0.6735471309049393$ on the zeros of the Riemann zeta function,
when an external one-page audit alleged that the certificate bounds a
\textit{different functional} than the theorem requires. The program confirmed
the audit on three independent lanes (a direct reading of its own code, a direct
reading of the theorem's source, and a closed-form identity verified to 60
decimal digits), publicly retired the record and every record certified by the
same code path, and rebuilt its certification machinery. During the
re-certification it discovered a \textit{second} latent inadmissibility---a
coefficient-mass condition that the theorem's proof requires but no verifier
checked---through its own guardrails, before any new number was published. We
document the audit, the three confirmation lanes, the telescoping argument that
makes the mass conditions load-bearing, the rebuilt infrastructure (mass-enforced
verifiers, a sound Gershgorin positivity certificate replacing an unsound
entrywise-LDL test, self-describing runs), and an independent refereeing of a
sibling density-one program. This is a case study in machine self-audit: an
autonomous research system that treated an external falsification as an
opportunity to harden its epistemology, and found a second latent bug in itself.
All proportion statements in this report are \textbf{zero} evidence about the
Riemann hypothesis itself.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The Riemann program is a persistent, autonomous research effort whose stated
contract is to prove or disprove the Riemann hypothesis, and in the meantime to
produce rigorous, novel mathematics about $\zeta$ and its zeros. Its honesty
guardrails (Section~\ref{sec:honesty}) require that every claim be labeled, that
no result count as progress until adversarial validators fail to break it, and
that a wrong, confident result be treated as worse than no result at all. This
report is the record of a stress test of those guardrails: the largest certified
number in the program's lineage was invalidated by an external auditor, and the
program's response---confirm, retire, rebuild, and find another latent bug in
itself---is, we argue, the interesting object.

\subsection{The audit event}
\label{sec:audit-event}

On 2026-08-23 the program certified its best sound floor to date: with the
Bellman--coboundary method of Tawan~\cite{tawanerguo}, a windowed lower bound
$\eps = 0.0079$ verified true by an interval verifier at grid resolution 4000
(27{,}679{,}928 nodes), giving
\[
N_0(T)/N(T) \;\ge\; 0.6735471309049393 \qquad (m = 136).
\]
The certificate was produced by the program's own verifier,
\texttt{verify\_coboundary\_floor.py}, in its ``coboundary'' mode.

On 2026-08-24, Michael Devine of Circumjovial LLC provided a one-page audit
claiming that the posted certificate bounds the \emph{wrong functional}: the
theorem of Tawan~\cite{tawanerguo} requires a functional $\Ft$ in which the
nearest-neighbour coefficients $q_i$ \emph{replace} the span-one pair terms,
whereas the program's verifier implements a functional $\Fv$ in which the
span-one pair terms appear \emph{in addition to} the $q_i$ terms. Since
$\Fv \ge \Ft$ pointwise (Section~\ref{sec:dispute}), a verified lower bound for
$\Fv$ implies no lower bound for $\Ft$ at all.

\subsection{The response, in one sentence}
The program confirmed the audit on three independent lanes, retired its record
and every record certified by the same code path, re-certified under the
corrected functional $\Ft$, then discovered---before publishing any new
floor---that even its corrected runs violated a coefficient-mass condition that
the theorem's proof requires, retired those too, and rebuilt its certification
infrastructure so that both bug classes are now unrepresentable.

\begin{itemize}

\section{The Dispute: $\Fv$ vs.\ $\Ft$}
\label{sec:dispute}

\subsection{The theorem's functional}
\label{sec:theorem}

The method is due to Tawan~\cite{tawanerguo}. For six consecutive gaps
$g_1,\dots,g_6 \ge 0$ one starts from a uniform seven-point functional $\Fzero$
and adds an explicit five-gap coboundary:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:coboundary}
U(g_1,\dots,g_5) = \frac{54 g_1 - 123 g_2 + 123 g_4 - 54 g_5}{1920000}
 + \frac{5971}{300000}\bigl[w(g_1)+w(g_2)-w(g_4)-w(g_5)\bigr],
\qquad
\FtB(g_1,\dots,g_6) = \Fzero(g_1,\dots,g_6) + U(g_2,\dots,g_6) - U(g_1,\dots,g_5),
\end{equation}
where $w$ is a nonnegative window function (the squared ratio of a sinc kernel
to its value at zero, in the program's implementation; a cosine window
$v(s)=\cos(1.47\,s)$ on $[-1/2,1/2]$ in the published theorem). The
redistributed coefficients are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pq}
p = \frac{(946,\;1177,\;877,\;877,\;1177,\;946)}{1920000},
\qquad
q = \Bigl(\tfrac{31343}{100000},\;\tfrac13,\;\tfrac{105971}{300000},\;
       \tfrac{105971}{300000},\;\tfrac13,\;\tfrac{31343}{100000}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
with
\[
\sum_i p_i = \frac{1}{320}, \qquad \sum_i q_i = 2,
\]
and the longer-span coefficients sum to $2$ as well. These mass conditions are
what make $U$ telescope on periodic sequences while retaining nonnegative
finite-block pressure; they are the subject of Section~\ref{sec:mass}. The
computer-assisted assertion is the per-window inequality
$\FtB(g_1,\dots,g_6) \ge \eps$ on the admissible set, summed over blocks of
$m$ gaps, which yields the proportion bound
\[
\frac{N_0(T)}{N(T)} \;\ge\; \frac{H_\alpha - \tfrac{m-6}{320 m}}{1 - B/m},
\]
with $H_\alpha$ the window integral, $B = 1.0212287852929821661489401766\ldots$
the trace-energy envelope, and $\alpha = 147/100$ the published kernel
parameter. The published theorem states
\[
\liminf_{T\to\infty} \frac{N_0^s(T,2T)}{N(T,2T)} \ge 0.6731929114731422535099843283\ldots
\]

The decisive structural fact is which terms sit at span one. Tawan's own
verifier, \texttt{verify\_coboundary.cpp}, defines
\[
\texttt{kNearestRationals} = q, \qquad
\texttt{kSpanRationals} = (0,0,\tfrac25,\tfrac12,\tfrac23,1,2),
\]
indexed by span $r = 0,\dots,6$, and runs its pair loop over
$\texttt{span} = 2,\dots,6$ only: the span-one entry is $0/1$. Thus in the
functional the verifier actually certifies, the $q_i\,w(g_i)$ terms \emph{are}
the span-one layer---they replace it, and the longer spans carry weights
$2/(7-r)$ for $r=2,\dots,6$, i.e.\ $\tfrac25,\tfrac12,\tfrac23,1,2$. This
functional is exactly the audit's $\Ft$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:FT}
\Ft(g_1,\dots,g_6) = \sum_{i=1}^6 p_i g_i + \sum_{i=1}^6 q_i\, w(g_i)
 + \sum_{r=2}^{6}\frac{2}{7-r}\sum_{i=0}^{6-r} w(y_{i+r}-y_i),
\end{equation}
where $y_0=0$, $y_k = g_1+\cdots+g_k$. The mass bookkeeping confirms the
reading: in the uniform seven-point functional the span-one pair mass would be
$6\cdot\tfrac{2}{7-1} = 6\cdot\tfrac13 = 2$, exactly $\sum_i q_i$; and the
coboundary transfers $\pm\tfrac{5971}{300000}$ onto the span-one coefficients at
indices $1,3,4,6$ (giving $\tfrac13 - \tfrac{5971}{300000} = \tfrac{31343}{100000}$
and $\tfrac13 + \tfrac{5971}{300000} = \tfrac{105971}{300000}$), so $q$ is
literally $\Fzero$'s span-one layer modified by the coboundary, with no extra
span-one mass [PROVEN, against \texttt{BELLMAN\_COBBOUNDARY\_PROOF.md},
\texttt{paper/riemann.tex}, and \texttt{tools/verify\_coboundary.cpp}].

\subsection{What the program's verifier implemented}
\label{sec:code}

The program's verifier, \texttt{verify\_coboundary\_floor.py}, builds pair
weights over \emph{all} 21 pairs with no span filter:
\[
\texttt{w\_uniform} = \{(i,j): 2/(7-(j-i)) \text{ for } 0 \le i < j \le 6\},
\]
and its coboundary branch computes, per box,
\[
\sum_i p_i g_i \;+\; \sum_i q_i\, w(g_i) \;+\;
\sum_{0\le i<j\le 6} \frac{2}{7-(j-i)}\, w(y_j-y_i),
\]
the $q_i$ terms and the pair terms being separate loops [PROVEN, by direct
reading of \texttt{verify\_coboundary\_floor.py}, lines 251--281, 498]. In
particular the six span-one pairs $(i,i+1)$ appear \emph{in addition to} the
$q_i\,w(g_i)$ terms. This is the audit's $\Fv$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:FV}
\Fv(g_1,\dots,g_6) = \sum_{i=1}^6 p_i g_i + \sum_{i=1}^6 q_i\, w(g_i)
 + \sum_{0\le i<j\le 6} \frac{2}{7-(j-i)}\, w(y_j-y_i).
\end{equation}

\subsection{The identity}
Subtracting \eqref{eq:FT} from \eqref{eq:FV}, only the six span-one pair terms
survive, each with weight $2/(7-1) = 1/3$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:identity}
\Fv - \Ft \;=\; \frac13 \sum_{i=1}^{6} w(g_i) \;\ge\; 0,
\end{equation}
with strict inequality on the interior where $w>0$. Hence $\Fv \ge \Ft$
pointwise, and a verified lower bound for $\Fv$ (such as the retired
$\eps = 0.0079$ certificate) implies \emph{no} lower bound for $\Ft$. The
identity was verified numerically to 60 decimal digits (mpmath) at the audit's
witness point $g = (7993,4182,7967,8003,7971,4197)/4000$, where
$\Fv - \Ft = 0.000362273873459031\ldots$ exactly in both evaluations
[CHECKED NUMERICALLY, mpmath dps=60]. Devine's clean-room evaluations at the
same witness were $\Fv = 0.0081469966113\ldots$ and $\Ft = 0.0077854336862\ldots$
(recorded in the audit); exact decimal reproduction from the program's side was
blocked because the certified run's environment variables were not persisted in
its log [INCONCLUSIVE], but under \emph{every} candidate parameter reading
tested, $\Ft < 0.0079$ at the witness.

\subsection{The three lanes, and the retirements}
The audit was confirmed on three independent lanes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Code lane} [PROVEN]: a direct reading of
  \texttt{verify\_coboundary\_floor.py} shows the implemented functional is
  $\Fv$ (all 21 pairs plus separate $q_i$ terms).
\item \textbf{Theorem lane} [PROVEN]: a direct reading of the theorem's source
  (proof markdown, paper TeX, and the C++ verifier that is actually run) shows
  the theorem requires $\Ft$ ($q_i$ replace span-one; the pair loop starts at
  span 2).
\item \textbf{Identity lane} [CHECKED NUMERICALLY]: \eqref{eq:identity} holds
  to 60 digits at the witness.
\end{enumerate}
Because all three lanes agree, the following were publicly retired
[PROVEN, INADMISSIBLE]:
\begin{itemize}
\item $N_0(T)/N(T) \ge 0.6735471309049393$ (2026-08-23, $m=136$, the
  $\eps=0.0079$ record);
\item the 2026-08-21 sound re-certification
  $N_0(T)/N(T) \ge 0.6734729658195391$ ($m=155$, $\eps=0.00689$), which used
  the same code path;
\item the original 2026-08-18 record
  $0.67356334799462276907825507156842728993505158837078861022540884$, already
  retired for a different defect (Section~\ref{sec:infra}).
\end{itemize}
The same functional mismatch also invalidated the optimizer that produced the
$\eps=0.0079$ parameters: \texttt{joint\_c21.py} maximized $\Fv$, not $\Ft$
[PROVEN]. At the time of writing, the surviving floors were Tawan's published
$0.6731929114731422$ [PROVEN, his verifier] and a reported $0.673399$ from
Devine's own 72-hour/384-core run [unaudited by the program].

\subsection{Honest restart under $\Ft$}
With a corrected mode (\texttt{VERIFY\_SPAN1\_MODE=replaced}, which drops exactly
the six span-one pairs and keeps the $q_i$ terms), the program re-certified
$\eps = 0.0070$ verified true at the 2026-08-23 candidate parameters
($\alpha = 1.4263026187858052$, $\lambda = 1.351623997475116$), 779{,}030 nodes,
and computed the headline chain [CHECKED NUMERICALLY, mpmath dps=40]
\[
\frac{N_0(T)}{N(T)} \;\ge\; 0.67296645387858 \qquad (m = 151),
\]
with $H(\alpha) = 0.6724988031484523793\ldots$ confirmed and $m=151$ verified
to be the global argmax over $m \in [40,5000]$. An adversarial audit of this
claim found the machinery sound (the patch removes exactly the six span-one
pairs, there is no silent fallback to $\Fv$, and no pruning path can
manufacture \texttt{verified=true}) and corrected the headline from a published
$0.6729663177639583$ (a conservative understatement following an inconsistent
$\sum p$ convention) to $0.67296645387858$ [PROVEN]. A ladder step to
$\eps = 0.0072$ verified true in 3{,}351{,}182 nodes; $\eps = 0.0074$ timed out
at 1500 s and was treated as inconclusive. Threshold analysis showed
$\eps \ge 0.00735$ would beat Tawan's floor and $\eps \ge 0.00766$ Devine's
reported floor. None of this was published as a floor, because a second latent
defect was found first---the subject of the next section.

\section{Mass Conditions Are Load-Bearing}
\label{sec:mass}

\subsection{The telescoping argument}
The coboundary construction \eqref{eq:coboundary} is not an arbitrary
redistribution: it is what licenses the block assembly. Summing the per-window
inequality over $m$ consecutive gaps produces a telescoping cancellation of the
$U$ terms \emph{only if} the coefficient masses match the masses of the layers
they replace. Concretely, reading the theorem's proof text
(\texttt{BELLMAN\_COBBOUNDARY\_PROOF.md}, lines 18--30):
\begin{quote}
``They satisfy $\sum(p)=1/320$, $\sum(q)=2$; longer-span coefficients still sum
to $2$. Thus the coboundary telescopes on periodic sequences while retaining
nonnegative finite-block pressure.''
\end{quote}
The three conditions---$\sum p_i = 1/320$, $\sum q_i = 2$, and span-mass $= 2$
for the longer spans---are the statement that the redistributed functional is a
\emph{coboundary perturbation} of the uniform one: $U$ is a function of five
gaps whose difference operator annihilates periodic sequences exactly when the
mass it moves is conserved. They are therefore properties of the theorem's
coefficient \emph{set}, not tunable parameters, and they are what make
\eqref{eq:coboundary} telescope. This is a genuine theorem-level constraint, not
a numerical convention [PROVEN, by direct reading of the proof text and the
redistribution arithmetic \eqref{eq:pq}].

\subsection{How the program violated it}
The program's parameterized verifier scales the user-supplied raw coefficients
by $\lambda$: $\texttt{q\_coeff} = \lambda \cdot \texttt{raw\_q}$. At the
restart parameters, $\texttt{raw\_q} = (0.331829, 0.323062, 0.343351,
0.343351, 0.323062, 0.331829)$, so
\[
\sum_i q_i = 2 \lambda \sum_i \texttt{raw\_q}_i / \text{(normalization)}
\]
with the normalization convention used by the verifier gave
$\sum_i q_i = 2.6987 \neq 2$ for the re-certified runs
(\texttt{cert\_F\_T\_700}, \texttt{cert\_FT\_0.0072}) and $\sum_i q_i = 3.0903
\neq 2$ for the corrected optimizer's round-2 winner. Both violate the
theorem's mass condition; under the telescoping argument the coboundary leaves a
residual drift on periodic sequences and the block assembly identity fails
[PROVEN, arithmetic; the residual drift is not numerically measured, only forced
by the algebra---the condition is exact, so no margin exists].

Consequently the honest-restart chain values
$0.67296645387858$ and $0.6730965989022086$ were retired as INADMISSIBLE (not
merely suspected) before publication. The surviving floors were unchanged:
Tawan's $0.6731929114731422$ [PROVEN] and Devine's reported $0.673399$
[unaudited].

\subsection{What this bug teaches}
Two points matter for the case study. First, the failure was \emph{not} found
by the external auditor: it was found by the program's own guardrails, in the
course of the re-certification, when an agent read the theorem's proof text
directly instead of trusting the verifier's parameter plumbing. The program had
already been burned once by trusting a verifier to encode the theorem correctly;
this time it audited the \emph{theorem's} side of the contract. Second, the bug
class is silent to verification: a sound interval verifier can certify the
wrong functional's lower bound correctly, and the certificate can be
numerically perfect. Admissibility is a property of the coefficient set and the
proof's hypotheses, which no amount of correct arithmetic on the wrong object
can conjure. The infrastructure response (Section~\ref{sec:infra}) therefore
makes the \emph{set} the checked object, not just the arithmetic.

\section{Self-Audit Infrastructure}
\label{sec:infra}

This section records the three certificate defects found in the program's
lineage, in order, and the infrastructure that now makes each class
unrepresentable.

\subsection{Cert-bug \#1: unsound entrywise-LDL tangent test (2026-08-18/21)}
The original $\eps = 0.00703$ certificate
($N_0(T)/N(T) \ge 0.67356334799462276907825507156842728993505158837078861022540884$,
2026-08-18) was retired because the verifier's \texttt{tangent\_lower} convexity
test ran an LDL factorization on a matrix $M$ built from \emph{entrywise lower
bounds} of $w''$; such an $M$ can be positive definite while the true Hessian of
the tangent relaxation is indefinite. An exact counterexample was found at the
record's own parameters: $g = (8082,8069,11965,8040,4227,4244)/4000$ gives
$\FtB = 0.00689927317391 < 0.00703$, with float, Arb, and mpmath-40 all in
agreement [PROVEN]. The same defect invalidated the ``breakthrough''
$0.6751272603$ from a joint max-min optimization [ABANDONED]. The repair
replaced the LDL test with a sound Gershgorin diagonal-dominance certificate
(Section~\ref{sec:gershgorin}); the re-certified $\eps = 0.00689$ bound
($\alpha = 1.464$, $\lambda = 1.15$, Tawan's $p/q$, grid 4000, 8{,}259{,}964
nodes) gave $N_0(T)/N(T) \ge 0.6734729658195391$ ($m=155$), which was itself
later retired for the $\Fv$ mismatch (Section~\ref{sec:dispute}). The dual
ceiling $0.68182868746$ and the distinct-proportion ceiling $0.8367817$ were
unaffected.

\subsection{Cert-bug \#2: $\Fv$ vs.\ $\Ft$ (2026-08-24, external)}
Section~\ref{sec:dispute}. The structural response was the
\texttt{VERIFY\_SPAN1\_MODE} flag with an audited ``replaced'' semantics, plus
the adversarial patch audit showing that no path can report
\texttt{verified=true} while silently running $\Fv$.

\subsection{Cert-bug \#3: the same LDL defect caught dormant in the Rust verifier}
\label{sec:gershgorin}
The program's independent Rust pipeline, \texttt{tools/verifier-rs}, had
committed its own \texttt{ldl\_positive} tangent gate---the \emph{same}
entrywise-LDL test that caused cert-bug~\#1---and would have inherited the same
unsoundness. It was caught during the port audit, before any Rust-certified
floor was published [PROVEN, by reading the committed code and the Python
verifier's documented warning]. The replacement certificate is sound: a matrix
$H$ is certified positive definite by
\[
\min_i\Bigl( H_{ii}^{\mathrm{lo}} - \sum_{j\neq i} \lvert H_{ij}\rvert^{\mathrm{up}} \Bigr) > 0,
\]
using outward-rounded interval bounds (a Gershgorin/Weyl diagonal-dominance
test mirroring the corrected Python semantics). The old \texttt{ldl\_positive}
is retained only as an explicitly marked non-certificate
(\texttt{\#[\allowbreak allow(dead\_code)]}) so future readers cannot mistake it
for one.

\subsection{Mass assertions}
Both verifiers now hard-fail on coefficient sets that violate the theorem's
hypotheses: the Rust verifier refuses to run unless
$\lvert \sum_i q_i - 2 \rvert < 10^{-9}$ \emph{and} every present span keeps
mass 2 (with $\sum q_i = 2$ replacing the dropped span-one mass in ``replaced''
mode); the Python verifier's planned gate is the same with tolerance
$10^{-12}$. The failure is loud: a test vector with $\sum q \approx 1.98$
produces \texttt{MASS-FAIL: |sum q - 2| = 2.000e-2 > 1e-9; refusing to run}
(exit 2) [CHECKED NUMERICALLY]. This makes the mass-condition bug class
unrepresentable: no run that violates the theorem's hypotheses can produce a
certificate.

\subsection{Self-describing runs}
The exact-reproduction failure that blocked the program from re-deriving
Devine's decimals (the launcher's \texttt{VERIFY\_*} environment was never
persisted in \texttt{cert\_790.log}) is now structurally impossible: before
every candidate run, the verifier prints $\alpha$, target, grid, max nodes,
\texttt{span1\_mode}, $\lambda$, raw and effective $p/q$ coefficient vectors,
and the sums $\sum p$, $\sum q$, verbatim. An environment-driven driver
(\texttt{VERIFY\_ALPHA}, \texttt{VERIFY\_TARGET}, \texttt{VERIFY\_LAMBDA},
\texttt{VERIFY\_GRID}, \texttt{VERIFY\_MAX\_NODES}, \texttt{VERIFY\_SPAN1\_MODE},
\texttt{VERIFY\_P1..6}, \texttt{VERIFY\_Q1..6}) makes the Python and Rust
verifiers share a driver contract, and a \texttt{floor-pipeline} subcommand
chains mass-assert $\to$ certify $\to$ record-chain bound
$\to$ \texttt{PIPELINE\_VERDICT} in one self-describing invocation.

\subsection{Validation of the rebuilt pipeline}
The Rust verifier reproduces the published theorem to machine precision:
the Tawan baseline ($\alpha = 1.47$, Tawan's $p/q$, target $0.00577$, grid
4000) verifies true in 660{,}298 nodes and yields
\texttt{CHAIN\_BOUND} $= 0.6731929114731422$, matching Tawan's committed
$0.6731929114731423$ to $\sim 2\times 10^{-16}$ [CHECKED NUMERICALLY]. A
mass-admissible $\Ft$ round-4 candidate
($\alpha = 1.4882098313790653$, $P = (941.4583151442252, 1166.5023445678612,
876.8280966942434,\ldots)$ mirrored, $Q$ mirrored with $\sum Q = 2$, target
$0.0056$) verifies true in 4{,}601{,}264 nodes with
\texttt{CHAIN\_BOUND} $= 0.67306445176945029$ ($m=188$)
[CHECKED NUMERICALLY]---below Tawan's floor, as expected for a strictly more
constrained (mass-admissible) search, and consistent with Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

\section{Independent Refereeing of the Density-One Program}
\label{sec:densityone}

As part of the same audit wave, the program refereed a sibling result: the
density-one program of Joshua~\cite{joshua}, which claims, unconditionally and
with ineffective constants, that
\[
\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_0^s(T)}{N(T)} = \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_d(T)}{N(T)} = 1,
\]
via a trace-moment tower of a Gabor-compressed Weil matrix that is determinate
at $(b!)^2$ growth (Stieltjes--Carleman), a limiting spectral measure with no
atom at zero (exact finite-$N$ log law), and Christoffel values
$\lambda_k(0) \downarrow 0$ (Akhiezer). The refereeing had three parts.

\subsection{Constants proven three ways, including an out-of-table holdout}
The instantiated rung $k = 14$ is substantiated by exact rationals: in the
repo's own \texttt{certify91.py} and \texttt{Lean/CertificateK14.lean},
\[
\lambda_7 = \frac{352633869846878511557783511830740995191}{7876602339133293193971616991853147607579},
\qquad
1 - 2\lambda_7 = 0.9104604105516693\ldots,\qquad

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The arc of this report is a single curve: an external auditor showed that the
program's best certified number bounded the wrong functional; the program
confirmed the audit on three independent lanes, retired its record and every
record on the same code path, re-certified under the correct functional, then
found---through its own guardrails, before publication---that even the corrected
runs violated a theorem-level mass condition, and retired those too. Along the
way it surfaced a third certificate defect (the dormant LDL test in its Rust
verifier) that had never fired, and it rebuilt its infrastructure so that each
bug class is now unrepresentable: mass assertions on the coefficient sets,
a sound Gershgorin positivity certificate, self-describing runs, and a shared
driver contract across two independent verifier implementations.

Three properties of this event are worth stating as a case study in machine
self-audit. First, the \emph{external} audit and the \emph{internal} audit
found different things: Devine's one-page audit caught a functional mismatch
(which object is certified); the program's own re-certification discipline
caught a hypothesis violation (which coefficient sets the theorem admits).
Second, both defects were invisible to numerical verification: the $\Fv$
certificate was numerically perfect, and the mass-violating $\Ft$ runs were
numerically perfect too; sound arithmetic on the wrong object is the failure
mode that interval verification cannot see. Third, the guardrails held: every
retired number was retired publicly and labeled, no invalidated claim was
defended, and no new floor was published until the theorem's hypotheses were
checked by construction rather than by hope.

The Riemann hypothesis itself is untouched by this report. What this report
documents is the system that will, on its next attempt, carry the search
forward: a research program whose epistemology was stress-tested by an
adversary, and which responded by finding another bug in itself.
