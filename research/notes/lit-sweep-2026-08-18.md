## Top candidates (the only two with any structural relevance)

### Candidate 1 — Holland, arXiv:2608.08682 (2026) [NEW, verified-by-fetch abstract]

**Theorem statement (quoted from abstract, PROVEN-in-literature):**
Let ξ(1/2+z)=Σ γ(n)/n! z^{2n}, J^{d,n}(X)=Σ_{j=0}^d C(d,j) γ(n+j) X^j. "We prove that there is
an absolute constant K>0 such that n³log²(n+2) ≥ K d⁵ ⟹ J^{d,n} is hyperbolic. Along every
sequence with n,d→∞ in this region, the empirical measure of the naturally centered and scaled
zeros also converges to Wigner's semicircle law."

**Why it escapes the S1 margin closure (assessed):** It is NOT a coefficient-margin theorem at
all. It is an analytic/spectral result on the γ(n) sequence (Hermite-expansion family) proving a
JOINT (n,d) hyperbolicity wedge with a Wigner limit — a different sufficient-condition family
than the killed margin class. It therefore does not hit the Newton-boundary closure head-on.

**Why it is NOT fundable as an RH one-way (honest):** It proves hyperbolicity of FINITE-degree
Jensen polynomials J^{d,n} in a wedge region. LP of Ξ (=RH) requires hyperbolicity for ALL
d,n≥0. A wedge theorem, however sharp, is the same finite-degree diagnostic class as GORZ/GORTTW
(which the ledger rules diagnostic-only — Jensen route diagnostic-only). It never crosses to the
all-d statement. It is a genuine new refinement, not a new sufficiency for LP of Ξ.

**Minimal bounded Rust probe (if funded as structural/record, NOT RH):** (1) Verify the wedge
for the campaign's real certified γ(n): pick (d,n) with n³log²(n+2) ≥ K d⁵ inside the region and
certify J^{d,n} hyperbolic (MPFR, reuse wave8d machinery); (2) determine the sharp effective K by
binary search at the wedge boundary; (3) the load-bearing question for a possible margin-link:
does Holland's mechanism, read on the coefficients, reduce to a margin-style inequality, or is it
genuinely a different (Hermite/semicircle) mechanism? If the latter, it is a structurally distinct
sufficiency family — but still finite-degree, so it cannot prove LP of Ξ. Forecast: INCONCLUSIVE
