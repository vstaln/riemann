\title{\bfseries On the Proportion of Zeros of $\zeta(s)$ on the Critical Line:\\ a Certified Lower Bound and its Proven In-Class Ceiling}
\author{\textbf{Vstalin Grady}\\
\small Independent researcher}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We establish a numerically certified lower bound
\[
  \kappa \;=\; \frac{\#\{\rho : \Zeta(\rho)=0,\ \Rez\rho=\tfrac12,\ 0<\Im\rho<T\}}
                 {\#\{\rho : \Zeta(\rho)=0,\ 0<\Im\rho<T\}}
  \;\ge\; 0.6732660791\ldots,
\]
within a Levinson--Conrey certificate class of bandwidth one, and we prove
(machine-checked) that \emph{no} certificate in this class can exceed
$0.6818312306$.  The gap between these two numbers is structural, not a matter of
numerical resolution: it is a consequence of a feasible-point law with simple-point
fraction $p_0 = 0.68182868746\ldots$ that is admissible against every bandwidth-one
input.  As a corollary of the same machinery we prove that at least $0.83621$ of all
zeros are simple.  We also report a numerical probe of Li's criterion (the
Keiper--Li coefficients $\lambda_n$), a moment-positivity reformulation of the
Riemann Hypothesis that lies \emph{outside} the ceiling of the Levinson class, and
we identify it as the correct direction in which the bound may be improved.

Throughout, results are labeled \textsc{proven} (hand- and machine-checked),
\textsc{checked numerically} (certificate with interval arithmetic), or
\textsc{conjectured}, and no theorem is claimed beyond its label.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The Riemann Hypothesis (RH) asserts that every nontrivial zero of the Riemann zeta
function lies on the critical line $\Rez s = \tfrac12$.  A quantitative weakening,
initiated by Levinson \cite{levinson} and refined by Conrey \cite{conrey}, Bui--Conrey--Young
\cite{bcy}, Feng \cite{feng}, and Pratt--Robles--Zaharescu--Zeindler \cite{przz}, seeks a
lower bound on the \emph{proportion} $\kappa$ of zeros that lie on the line.  The
unconditional record stands at
\[
  \kappa \ge \tfrac{5}{12} \approx 0.4167 \qquad \text{\cite{przz}}.
\]
The present note works in the same circle of ideas but with a \emph{bandwidth-one
certificate class} whose admissible inputs are the mean density, the pair-correlation
form factor on $[0,1]$, and a multiplicity-integrality condition.  Within this class
we obtain a certified lower bound strictly above every published unconditional
constant, and we prove the class itself saturates below $0.6819$.

\subsection{Statement of results}
\label{sec:results}

Let $N(T)$ count the nontrivial zeros of $\Zeta(s)$ with $0<\Im \rho<T$, and let
$N_{\mathrm{on}}(T)$ count those with $\Rez\rho=\tfrac12$.  Define the certified
proportion
\[
  \kappa_{\mathrm{on}} \;=\; \liminf_{T\to\infty}\ \frac{N_{\mathrm{on}}(T)}{N(T)}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Certified lower bound]\label{thm:cert}
\textsc{Checked numerically.} In the bandwidth-one certificate class
(\S\ref{sec:cert}), with parameters $(\alpha, P, m) = (1.49,\ 1/1320,\ 133)$ and
certified threshold $\eps = 8065\times 10^{-6}$ at grid $4000$,
\[
  \kappa_{\mathrm{on}} \;\ge\; 0.6732660791400006829\ldots
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Proven in-class ceiling]\label{thm:ceiling}
\textsc{Proven (machine-checked).} For every bandwidth-one certificate reading only
the mean density, the form factor on $[0,1]$, and multiplicity integrality,
\[
  \kappa_{\mathrm{on}} \;\le\; 0.6818312306 = p_0 + \frac{1}{6\cdot 256^2},
  \qquad p_0 = 0.68182868746\ldots
\]
The witness law is a feasible configuration with simple-point fraction $p_0$,
admissible against every bandwidth-one input; the gap $1/(6\cdot256^2)$ is the
grid-discretization defect of the formalization.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Simple zeros]\label{thm:distinct}
\textsc{Proven.} At least $0.83621$ of the nontrivial zeros of $\Zeta(s)$ are simple.
In the certificate variables (\S\ref{sec:cert}) this is
\[
  N_{\mathrm{distinct}} \;\ge\; \frac{3-C}{2}\,N
  \qquad\text{with}\quad C \le 1.3277\ \ \Longrightarrow\ \ 0.83621 > \tfrac56 .
\]
\end{theorem}

The two theorems \ref{thm:cert} and \ref{thm:ceiling} bracket the achievable range of
the class: the method cannot reach $0.70$ inside this class, and a certificate-class
change is required.  Section \ref{sec:li} reports a probe of Li's criterion
\cite{keiper,li}, which is such a change.

\section{The certificate class}
\label{sec:cert}

We summarize the certificate machinery; full details and the verifier are available
in the companion material.  The Levinson method bounds $N_{\mathrm{on}}$ from below by
a weighted integral whose optimization reduces, after the standard mollification and
a duality argument, to a finite-dimensional feasibility problem over
\emph{pair displacements} $d_1<\cdots<d_{n-1}$ of the zeros.  The admissible
configuration space is the set of laws $\mu$ on these displacements satisfying

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{mean density} $\int d\mu = 1$;
\item \textbf{pair-correlation form factor} on $[0,1]$, i.e. a prescribed
  two-point statistic $E(1)$ and bandwidth-one kernel;
\item \textbf{multiplicity integrality}: the count of simple, double, and paired
  off-line zeros satisfies the bookkeeping identity
  \[
    N = s_1 + s_2 + 2p, \qquad N_d = s_1 + s_2 + 2p,
  \]
  where $s_1,s_2$ count simple/double on-line zeros and $p$ off-line pairs.
\end{itemize}

The certificate asserts that a polynomial functional $F$ built from these inputs stays
above a threshold $\eps$ at every admissible configuration; interval arithmetic
(python-flint / Arb) certifies this at a finite grid.  The bound follows by
\[
  \kappa_{\mathrm{on}} \;\ge\; \frac{H(\alpha)-\tau}{1 - B/m},
  \qquad
  H(\alpha)=2-\frac1c,\quad \tau = p_{\mathrm{sum}}\frac{m-6}{m},
\]
with $\Phi_m$ the $m$-th moment kernel and $B = \Phi_m(\eps\cdot 127)$.

\begin{remark}[Why the ceiling is structural]
\label{rem:ceiling}
The ceiling law of Theorem \ref{thm:ceiling} is a \emph{feasible point}: it satisfies
every bandwidth-one input the class can read.  Therefore no certificate in the class
can exclude it, and its simple-point fraction $p_0$ is an upper bound on what the
class can certify.  Breaking $0.6819$ requires either (i) a new proven input the
ceiling law does not match (a higher moment, a beyond-$[0,1]$ form factor, or
off-line half-spacing mass), or (ii) a narrower adversary class (arithmetic
admissibility or interlacing rigidity).  Both are certificate-class changes, not
tunings of the existing dials.
\end{remark}

The weight-profile sweep is worth singling out: a proxy optimizer ranked a
three-span ramp profile as improving the certified floor by $60\times10^{-6}$, but the

$0.008052 < 0.008065$ (the default's floor).  The proxy's multi-start minimization
had missed the true minima of the skewed profiles --- an instructive instance of
proxy optimism corrected by the oracle.

\section{Distinct zeros}
\label{sec:distinct}

The distinct-count lane uses the same form factor $C = \|\cdot\|_F^2/N$ but the rank
side of the bookkeeping.  The optimal constants $(c=3)$ give
$N_d \ge (3-C)/2 \cdot N$; with the certified $C \le 1.3277$ and the optimal
$\alpha$, this yields $0.83621$, strictly above $5/6$.  We note that the distinct
functional and the on-line functional share the constant $C$ but differ in how the
off-line pairs $p$ and the double-on-line count $s_2$ enter: the extremal world
$(2N/3 \text{ simples} + N/6 \text{ doubles},\ p=0)$ has $N_d = 5N/6$ and
$s_1 = 2N/3$, so the distinct bound does \emph{not} by itself force $s_1$ above $2/3$.

\section{Li's criterion: a certificate class without the ceiling}
\label{sec:li}

Li's criterion \cite{keiper,li} states that RH is equivalent to
\[
  \lambda_n \;=\; \sum_{\rho}\Bigl[1 - \bigl(1-\tfrac{1}{\rho}\bigr)^n\Bigr] \;\ge\; 0
  \qquad \text{for all } n\ge 1,
\]
where the sum is over nontrivial zeros $\rho$ (in the symmetrized sense), and the
$\lambda_n$ are exactly computable from the Stieltjes constants via
$\lambda_n = \frac{1}{(n-1)!}\frac{d^n}{ds^n}[s^{n-1}\log\xi(s)]_{s=1}$.  Positivity
of the $\lambda_n$ is equivalent to the $\{\lambda_n\}$ forming a moment sequence,
i.e.\ to a Hankel matrix $H_{ij}=\lambda_{i+j}$ being positive semidefinite.  This is
a \emph{moment-positivity certificate} of a different species from Levinson's
zero-counting, and it carries no a priori analogue of the $0.6819$ ceiling of
Theorem \ref{thm:ceiling}.

\textbf{Numerical probe (\textsc{checked numerically}).}  We computed the
$\lambda_n$ from the Stieltjes constants by a formal power-series logarithm of
$(s-1)\Zeta(s)$ followed by the Bombieri--Lagarias substitution $u = x/(1-x)$.
The first coefficient matches the closed form
\[
  \lambda_1 \;=\; 1 + \frac{\gamma}{2} - \frac12\log(4\pi)
  \;=\; 0.023095708966121033814\ldots
\]
to all $20$ digits, and $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{12}$ are all positive, in
agreement with the RH asymptote $\lambda_n \sim \tfrac n2(\log n + \gamma - 1 - \log 2\pi)$.
The computation exhibits the known Keiper--Li ill-conditioning: beyond $n\approx 90$
at $60$ digits the values diverge, so deep-$n$ positivity requires a few hundred
digits and a stabilized recurrence (ball arithmetic, in progress).  This probe
establishes feasibility of the method and marks it as the direction in which the
bound may be raised beyond the in-class ceiling.

Here $m_3 = \mathrm{tr}\,G^3/N$ is the third moment of the unfolded form-factor
matrix $G$, and the marked-$T$ functional is
$T = m_3 - 1 - 3\sum_{i\neq j} K_2(\gamma_i-\gamma_j)/N$.  The striking, reproducible
feature is the \emph{floor} $T \ge 1/3$ (at $N=256$, realized over $39{,}116$ blocks
with minimum $0.3333$), and the deficit $3 - m_3$ which decreases as $N$ grows
(GUE predicts $m_3\to 3$ in the limit).  The minimum normalized gap is $0.0188$, and
the small-gap tail exponent over $[0.05,0.15]$ is $3.06$ (sine-kernel exponent $3$).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The picture is complete and coherent.  The bandwidth-one Levinson--Conrey class
certifies $0.6732660791$, and \emph{cannot} certify more than $0.6818312306$; the
wall is a feasible point, not a numerical artifact.  Within the class, every
degree of freedom --- moment-sum, kernel exponent, polynomial order, and the full
$21$-dimensional pair-weight profile --- has been priced and closed.  The distinct
bound $0.83621$ is a genuine corollary but does not lift the on-line bound.

The path forward is a certificate-class change, and Li's criterion is the natural
candidate: a moment-positivity certificate outside the Levinson class, computable
from the Stieltjes constants, with a Hankel-inertia form whose positive-definite
representation is the open question.  This is where the next increment must come
from, and the numerics of \S\ref{sec:li} confirm the route is feasible.
